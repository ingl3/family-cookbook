Our family

To my children

There are few things in life more important than family. No matter where life takes you, how much you accomplish, or how far from home you travel, never forget the people who helped shape who you are. Some of life’s greatest memories are made around a kitchen table. It is where we laugh, celebrate, disagree, forgive, share our stories, and make plans for the future. Food has a remarkable way of bringing us together, even when life pulls us in different directions. I want each of you to grow into someone who is independent, capable, generous and grounded. Work hard for what you want. Keep your word. Take responsibility for your decisions. Treat people with respect, regardless of what they have or what they can do for you. Be courageous enough to fail and disciplined enough to try again. Never become so busy building a life that you forget to live it. Learn to cook not only because it is an essential life skill, but because preparing a meal for someone is one of the simplest and most meaningful ways to show love. When you have more than you need, share it. When someone needs a seat at your table, make room. When life becomes difficult, remember that you do not have to face everything alone. My hope is that this cookbook travels with you through every chapter of your life. That its pages become stained, its corners become worn, and its margins become filled with your own notes, improvements and memories. Add your own recipes. Create your own traditions. Teach your children what you have learned. Keep building upon what our family has started. Success is not measured solely by what we accumulate, but by what we contribute, the people we love, and what we leave behind. Wherever you go, whatever you become, there will always be a place for you at my table. Happy Thanksgiving. With all my love, Dad Thanksgiving Day, November 26, 2026

The Hands That Fed Us

Our heritage. Our history. Our people.

Before you turn these pages and begin cooking, I want you to understand that your family story began long before you arrived. Your roots run deep and wide. Within you lives a rich heritage of African, Indigenous Native American and Taíno ancestry. You come from people who worked the land, crossed great distances, served their country, raised families and found ways to create abundance even when they had very little. Food has always been part of our family’s story. Every generation has contributed something to our table, and it is important that we remember the hands that fed us.

Charles Mozell — Service, Courage & Sacrifice

My maternal grandfather, Charles Mozell, my mother’s father, was a World War II veteran who joined the United States Navy at just sixteen years old, serving as a cook aboard a naval warship. At an age when most young people are still discovering who they are, he was already serving his country and feeding the men who served alongside him. His story reminds us that preparing a meal is an act of service, that every contribution has value, and that courage comes in many forms.

Charles Andrews & Aunt Jean — From the Soil to the Soul

Charles Andrews, a decorated Army veteran, was a direct descendant of our sharecropping ancestors who settled in Oklahoma and followed the railroad to California in the early 1900s. Their journey represents generations of perseverance, sacrifice and the pursuit of greater opportunity. Together with his loving wife, my Aunt Jean, Charles enjoyed backyard farming long before it was fashionable. Growing your own food, working with your hands and appreciating what the earth provides were simply a way of life. They remind us that wealth is also measured in what you can grow, make and share.

A Legacy of Love

The stories and flavors we carry forward.

Auntie Rochelle — A Larger-than-Life Spirit

Standing at a mere four feet eight inches tall, Auntie Rochelle carried an enormous presence and brought our Louisiana roots to California. She loved cooking for our family, serving generous portions of food alongside equally generous portions of stories. Her kitchen was a place where our history was spoken, laughter was shared and family bonds were strengthened. Never underestimate the power of a good meal, a good story and a house full of family.

Desiree Diggs — The Sweetness of Family

Our resident baker, Desiree Diggs, made a famous 7UP pound cake that earned a permanent place on everyone’s most-requested list. Some recipes become the taste of a celebration and the anticipation of a family gathering. The gifts we share with our family become memories they carry for a lifetime.

Skylar White-Anderson — Keeping the Flame Alive

My cousin by birth, my sister by heart, Skylar White-Anderson is one of the most amazing cooks I know. From her incredible Cajun pasta to her famous lamb chops (borrowed from me!), she carries our family traditions forward with her own creativity. Whether it is her kitchen classics, desserts, drinks or sides, she has a gift for bringing people together through food. She represents our heritage in motion, honoring the generations before us while creating traditions of her own. Learn from her, cook alongside her and celebrate the talent she brings to our family. Just remember who taught her about those lamb chops.

To the Many Hands Behind Our Family

To the mothers, grandmothers, great-grandmothers, fathers, grandfathers, aunts and uncles whose names may not appear individually here: your contributions are no less important. To those who stretched a meal to feed one more person, worked long days, raised children, planted gardens, passed down recipes and kept our family connected — this book belongs to you, too. No family legacy is built by a few people alone. It is built by generations of hands, hearts and voices. We honor them all.

Our family

A Little Kitchen Wisdom

You don’t need much to make something great.

01. The Family Rule

Nobody leaves our table hungry. Pull up another chair. Read the recipe first, clean as you go, taste when safe and leave the kitchen better than you found it.

02. A Pantry With Roots

Rice, beans, black-eyed peas, pigeon peas, cornmeal, grits, flour and sweet potatoes. Keep onions, garlic, peppers, celery, thyme, cilantro and culantro handy. Bring flavor with sofrito, adobo, sazón, annatto, Creole seasoning, cumin, oregano, cayenne, allspice and hot sauce. Think collards, okra, plantains, yuca, coconut milk, citrus, chiles and fresh garden vegetables. Add oil, butter, eggs, broth and imagination. Build your pantry around what you actually eat.

03. A Few Good Tools Go a Long Way

A sharp knife, cutting board, cast-iron skillet, pot with a lid, baking pan, mixing bowl, wooden spoon and a food thermometer. A fork can mash potatoes; your hands can mix dough. You don’t need a cabinet full of gadgets to make your kitchen shine.

04. A Little of This, a Little of That

Add a little, taste it when safe, and adjust. Watch the consistency when mixing wet and dry ingredients: a batter should pour when the recipe calls for it, while a dough may need to hold its shape. Baking needs more care with proportions. Start with the recipe, then let experience, observation and common sense guide you.

05. Keep Your Kitchen Clean and Food Safe

Wash hands, clean as you go and separate raw meat from ready-to-eat foods. Cook poultry and leftovers to 165°F, ground beef to 160°F, fish and whole beef cuts to 145°F (rest beef 3 minutes). Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours, or 1 hour above 90°F. Never taste raw meat or uncooked flour or egg batter.

06. Waste Less, Create More

Yesterday’s safely stored rice can become fried rice. Leftover chicken can become soup. Ripe bananas can become banana bread. Work with what you have, make it your own, and write down the dishes everyone asks for again.

FIRE & SMOKE / GRILLING

Dad’s Brisket

Inspired by Uncle Cleve Baker

SERVES / YIELD • 8–12 | 30 min prep | 10–16 hr cook

Ingredients

  • 1 whole brisket (10–14 lb)

  • Kosher salt

  • Coarse black pepper

  • Garlic powder

  • Oak or hickory wood

Method

  1. Trim fat cap to about ¼ inch. Season generously with salt, pepper and garlic powder.

  2. Set smoker for indirect heat at 225–250°F. Smoke fat side toward the heat until bark is set, usually around 165–175°F internal.

  3. Wrap in butcher paper or foil if desired. Continue until a probe slides in easily, often 195–205°F.

  4. Rest wrapped for at least 1 hour, keeping above 140°F if holding longer. Slice against the grain.

Kitchen wisdom

Let tenderness, not the clock, tell you when brisket is ready.

Picanha

SERVES / YIELD • 4–6 | 15 min prep | 25 min cook

Ingredients

  • 2–3 lb picanha (top sirloin cap)

  • Coarse kosher salt

  • Black pepper, optional

Method

  1. Leave the fat cap intact. Cut into thick steaks with the grain so finished slices can be cut against it.

  2. Salt generously. Grill over indirect medium heat, then sear over direct heat, watching for fat flare-ups.

  3. Cook to 145°F and rest at least 3 minutes. Slice against the grain and serve.

Kitchen wisdom

The fat cap is part of the flavor; don’t trim it all away.

Backyard Burgers

SERVES / YIELD • 4 | 10 min prep | 12 min cook

Ingredients

  • 1½ lb ground beef

  • 1 tsp kosher salt

  • ½ tsp pepper

  • 4 burger buns

  • 4 slices cheddar

  • lettuce, tomato and pickles

  • 2 tbsp mayonnaise

Method

  1. Divide beef into four patties and press a shallow dimple into each. Season outside.

  2. Grill over medium-high heat, flipping once, until 160°F. Add cheese in the last minute.

  3. Toast buns and assemble with toppings.

Kitchen wisdom

Avoid pressing patties on the grill; it squeezes out juices.

RISE & SHINE / BAKING

Buttermilk Biscuits

SERVES / YIELD • 8 | 15 min prep | 15 min bake

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour

  • 1 tbsp baking powder

  • ¼ tsp baking soda

  • ¾ tsp salt

  • 6 tbsp cold unsalted butter

  • ¾ cup cold buttermilk

Method

  1. Heat oven to 425°F. Whisk dry ingredients and cut in butter until pea-sized.

  2. Stir in buttermilk just until combined. Pat dough to ¾ inch thick, fold in thirds twice and pat again.

  3. Cut eight biscuits, place on a lined sheet and bake 12–16 minutes until golden.

Kitchen wisdom

Cold butter and gentle handling make flaky layers.

Southern Sausage Gravy

SERVES / YIELD • 4–6 | 5 min prep | 20 min cook

Ingredients

  • 1 lb breakfast sausage

  • ¼ cup flour

  • 2½–3 cups whole milk

  • Black pepper

  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. Brown sausage in a skillet, breaking it into small pieces

  2. cook to 160°F.

  3. Leave about 3 tbsp drippings in the pan, adding butter if needed. Stir in flour and cook 1–2 minutes.

  4. Gradually stir in milk. Simmer, stirring, until gravy coats a spoon. Season with pepper and salt.

  5. Serve over warm split biscuits.

Kitchen wisdom

Add milk a splash at a time until the gravy is just right.

Crispy Hash Browns

SERVES / YIELD • 4 | 15 min prep | 15 min cook

Ingredients

  • 2 large russet potatoes

  • 2–3 tbsp oil

  • 1 tbsp butter

  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Grate potatoes, rinse in cold water and squeeze very dry in a clean towel.

  2. Heat oil and butter in a skillet over medium-high heat. Spread potatoes in a thin layer.

  3. Cook undisturbed until golden, about 5–7 minutes. Flip in sections and brown the other side.

  4. Season and serve hot.

Kitchen wisdom

Dry potatoes and a little patience make the crispiest hash browns.

Mama’s Cream of Wheat

In honor of my grandmother, Mattie Davis

SERVES / YIELD • 4 | 5 min prep | 10 min cook

Ingredients

  • ¾ cup Cream of Wheat (farina)

  • 4 cups milk or water

  • 2 tbsp butter

  • Sugar to taste

  • Pinch of salt

  • Vanilla, cinnamon or nutmeg

  • (optional)

Method

  1. Warm milk or water with a pinch of salt over medium heat.

  2. Slowly sprinkle in cereal, stirring continuously to avoid lumps.

  3. Reduce heat and cook, stirring, until creamy, following the package timing.

  4. Stir in butter and sugar to taste. Add vanilla or spices if desired.

  5. If too thick, add a splash of warm milk. Serve warm.

Kitchen wisdom

Don’t rush it. Keep stirring and watch the consistency. A warm bowl of something homemade can carry as much love as an elaborate Sunday dinner.

SET IT & ROAST IT / OVEN FAVORITES

Sunday Rotisserie Chicken

SERVES 4–6 | 15 min prep | 60–90 min cook

Ingredients

  • 1 whole chicken, 4–5 lb

  • 2 tbsp olive oil

  • 2 tbsp softened butter

  • Salt, pepper, garlic powder

  • Lemon, thyme or rosemary

  • OR 1 cooked grocery rotisserie

  • chicken

Method

  1. For homemade: preheat grill with rotisserie to 350–375°F. Pat chicken dry, season with oil, salt, pepper and garlic; tuck wings and tie legs.

  2. Secure on spit, balance and rotate over indirect heat with a drip pan until breast and innermost thigh and wing reach 165°F, about 60–90 minutes. Rest 10 minutes.

  3. Shortcut: grab a cooked chicken from Costco or your grocer. Quarter it, brush with seasoned butter and place skin-side up on a baking sheet.

  4. Roast at 425°F for 10–20 minutes until skin crisps and chicken reaches 165°F if reheating. Watch closely; broil briefly if needed.

Kitchen wisdom

A store-bought chicken is a head start, not a compromise. Save the bones for stock.

Layla’s Famous Baked Mac & Cheese

SERVES 6–8 | 20 min prep | 25–30 min bake

Ingredients

  • 1 lb elbow macaroni

  • 4 tbsp butter

  • ¼ cup flour

  • 3 cups whole milk

  • 3 cups sharp cheddar, shredded

  • 1 cup Monterey Jack, shredded

  • ½ tsp mustard powder

  • Salt and pepper to taste

  • Extra cheese for topping

  • Optional: 1 lb lobster meat, cooked

  • Optional: 2 tbsp butter for lobster

Method

  1. Heat oven to 375°F. Cook macaroni 2 minutes less than package directions; drain.

  2. Melt butter, whisk in flour for 1 minute, then gradually whisk in milk. Simmer until thickened. Remove from heat and stir in cheeses and seasoning.

  3. For lobster mac: gently warm cooked lobster in butter, cut into bite-size pieces and fold into the macaroni with the cheese sauce.

  4. Transfer to a greased 9 × 13-inch dish, top with extra cheese and bake 20–30 minutes until bubbling and golden. Rest 5 minutes.

Kitchen wisdom

Layla makes the best mac and cheese ever. Grate your own cheese and keep the sauce creamy before baking.

Slow-Braised Oxtails

SERVES 4–6 | 25 min prep | 3–4 hr cook

Ingredients

  • 3 lb oxtails

  • 1 onion, chopped

  • 4 garlic cloves

  • 2 carrots, chopped

  • 2 cups beef broth, plus more as

  • needed

  • 1 tbsp Worcestershire

  • 1–2 tsp browning sauce, optional

  • Thyme, allspice, salt, pepper

  • 1 tbsp oil

  • 1 can butter beans, optional

Method

  1. Season oxtails and brown well in oil in a heavy oven-safe pot. Remove.

  2. Sauté onion, carrots and garlic. Add broth, Worcestershire, browning sauce and herbs; scrape up browned bits. No tomato paste.

  3. Return oxtails; liquid should come about halfway up. Cover and braise at 325°F for 3–4 hours until fork-tender.

  4. Skim excess fat. Add drained butter beans for the last 20 minutes if desired. Reduce gravy uncovered if needed, taste and serve with rice.

Kitchen wisdom

Deep browning, patience and good seasoning build the gravy.

Spaghetti with Meat Sauce

SERVES / YIELD • 6 | 15 min prep | 35 min cook

Ingredients

  • 1 lb spaghetti

  • 1 lb ground beef

  • 1 onion, diced

  • 3 garlic cloves, minced

  • 1 tbsp olive oil

  • 28 oz crushed tomatoes

  • 2 tbsp tomato paste

  • 1 tsp Italian seasoning

  • ½ tsp salt

  • Parmesan to serve

Method

  1. Heat oil in a pot; soften onion 5 minutes. Add beef and cook to 160°F, breaking it up. Drain excess fat.

  2. Add garlic, tomato paste, tomatoes and seasoning. Simmer 25 minutes.

  3. Cook spaghetti in salted water according to package. Drain and toss with sauce; top with Parmesan.

Kitchen wisdom

A little pasta water helps sauce cling to noodles.

Brown Stew Chicken & Rice

SERVES / YIELD • 4–6 | 25 min prep | 45 min cook

Ingredients

  • 2½ lb chicken thighs or drumsticks

  • 1 onion

  • 1 bell pepper

  • 3 garlic cloves

  • Scallions and thyme

  • 1 tbsp browning sauce

  • 1 tbsp brown sugar

  • 1 tsp allspice

  • 1 cup chicken broth

  • 2 tbsp oil

  • Salt, pepper, hot pepper

  • 2 cups rice

Method

  1. Season chicken with browning sauce, sugar, allspice, salt and pepper. Brown in oil, in batches.

  2. Sauté chopped onion, pepper, garlic and scallions. Add broth and thyme

  3. return chicken.

  4. Cover and simmer 30–40 minutes until tender and at least 165°F. Uncover to thicken gravy

  5. adjust seasoning.

  6. Cook rice separately according to package directions and spoon chicken and gravy over it.

Kitchen wisdom

Build color slowly; browned is flavorful, burnt is bitter.

Creamy Garlic Chicken & Broccoli Pasta

SERVES / YIELD • 4–6 | 15 min prep | 25 min cook

Ingredients

  • 12 oz pasta

  • 1 lb chicken breast, cubed

  • 3 cups broccoli florets

  • 4 garlic cloves

  • 2 tbsp butter

  • 1 cup heavy cream

  • ¾ cup Parmesan

  • Salt, pepper, oil

Method

  1. Boil pasta in salted water

  2. add broccoli for the final 3 minutes. Reserve a cup of pasta water and drain.

  3. Season chicken and sauté in oil until 165°F. Set aside.

  6. taste and adjust.

A splash of pasta water helps the sauce cling to every bite.

Dad’s Lamb Chop Lollipops

SERVES 4 | 15 min prep | 10–15 min cook

Ingredients

  • 2 racks of lamb, frenched, cut into chops

  • 2 tbsp olive oil

  • 3 tbsp butter

  • 4 garlic cloves, crushed

  • 2 sprigs rosemary

  • Salt and cracked black pepper

  • Optional: squeeze of lemon

Method

  1. Pat chops dry. Season with salt, pepper and a little oil. Preheat the oven broiler.

  2. Heat cast iron over medium-high. Sear chops on both sides until rare inside.

  3. Add butter, garlic and rosemary. Baste briefly, then transfer the oven-safe skillet under the broiler.

  4. Broil briefly, watching closely, until browned and 145°F internally. Rest 3 minutes; finish with lemon.

Kitchen wisdom

Borrowed by Skylar, approved by the family. A hot skillet and a good crust make all the difference.

Skillet Cornbread

SERVES / YIELD • 8 | 10 min prep | 22 min bake

Ingredients

  • 1 cup yellow cornmeal

  • 1 cup flour

  • 1 tbsp baking powder

  • ½ tsp salt

  • ¼ cup sugar

  • 1 cup buttermilk

  • 2 eggs

  • ¼ cup melted butter

  • 1 tbsp oil

Method

  1. Heat oven to 425°F with a 10-inch cast-iron skillet inside.

  2. Mix dry ingredients. Whisk buttermilk, eggs and butter; combine gently.

  3. Carefully oil hot skillet, pour in batter and bake 18–23 minutes until golden and a toothpick comes out clean.

Kitchen wisdom

Always use oven mitts: the handle stays hot.

Steakhouse Filet with Garlic Shrimp

SERVES / YIELD • 4 | 15 min prep | 20 min cook

Ingredients

  • 4 filet mignon steaks

  • 1 lb peeled shrimp

  • 4 tbsp butter

  • 4 garlic cloves

  • Oil, salt, pepper

  • Lemon and parsley

Method

  1. Pat steaks dry and season. Heat cast iron with a little oil. Sear steaks on both sides, then lower heat or finish in a 400°F oven until 145°F.

  2. Rest steaks at least 3 minutes. Reduce skillet heat and add butter and garlic.

  3. Add shrimp and cook until opaque and 145°F, about 2 minutes per side depending on size.

  4. Finish with lemon and parsley. Spoon garlic shrimp and butter over steaks.

Kitchen wisdom

Cook the shrimp last so it stays tender.

Dad’s Herb Butter Smoked Turkey

SERVES 8–12 | 30 min prep | 2–5 hr cook

Ingredients

  • 1 turkey, 12–14 lb, fully thawed

  • ½ cup softened butter

  • 4 garlic cloves, minced

  • Rosemary, thyme and sage

  • Salt and black pepper

  • 1 lemon and 1 onion

  • A little oil

  • Apple, pecan or cherry smoking

  • wood

Method

  1. Set grill or smoker for indirect cooking at 325–350°F. Add a little smoking wood. Mix butter with garlic, herbs and seasoning. Pat turkey dry; rub butter under skin and oil the outside.

  2. Whole bird: tuck wings, place onion and lemon loosely in cavity and cook breast-up over a drip pan, roughly 3–5 hours.

  3. Spatchcock: remove backbone with poultry shears, turn breast-up and press flat. Cook skin-up over indirect heat, roughly 2–3 hours.

  4. For either method, verify 165°F in thickest breast and innermost thigh and wing. Rest 20–30 minutes before carving. Cook stuffing separately.

Kitchen wisdom

Keep the lid closed, use clean light smoke and trust the thermometer rather than the clock.

Creamy Mashed Potatoes

SERVES / YIELD • 8 | 15 min prep | 25 min cook

Ingredients

  • 3 lb Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled

  • and cubed

  • 6 tbsp butter

  • ¾ cup warm milk

  • ½ cup sour cream

  • 1 tsp salt, plus more for water

  • ½ tsp pepper

Method

  1. Cover potatoes with cold salted water. Boil gently 15–20 minutes until fork-tender.

  2. Drain well and return to warm pot for 1 minute to evaporate moisture.

  3. Mash with butter, milk and sour cream. Season to taste.

Kitchen wisdom

Avoid a blender; it can make potatoes gummy.

Sweet Potato Soufflé

SERVES / YIELD • 8 | 25 min prep | 35 min cook

Ingredients

  • 3 lb sweet potatoes

  • ½ cup butter, melted

  • ½ cup brown sugar

  • 2 eggs

  • ½ cup milk

  • 1 tsp vanilla

  • Cinnamon, nutmeg, salt

  • ½ cup chopped pecans

  • ¼ cup flour

  • ¼ cup brown sugar

  • 2 tbsp butter

Method

  1. Bake or boil peeled sweet potatoes until tender

  2. drain and mash. Let cool slightly.

  3. Mix with melted butter, sugar, eggs, milk, vanilla and spices. Spread in a buttered baking dish.

  4. Mix pecans, flour, remaining sugar and butter into crumbs

  5. scatter over top.

  6. Bake at 350°F for 30–40 minutes until set and center reaches 160°F.

Kitchen wisdom

Taste the sweet potato mixture before adding eggs; adjust sweetness to your liking.

Rosemary & Herb Stuffing

SERVES 8 | 20 min prep | 40–50 min bake

Ingredients

  • 1 lb day-old bread, cubed

  • ½ cup butter

  • 1 onion, diced

  • 3 celery stalks, diced

  • 2 tbsp fresh rosemary, chopped

  • 1 tbsp fresh sage, chopped

  • 1 tbsp fresh thyme

  • 2–3 cups chicken or turkey stock

  • 2 eggs, beaten

  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Heat oven to 350°F. If bread is fresh, toast cubes until dry.

  2. Sauté onion and celery in butter until soft. Stir in rosemary, sage and thyme.

  3. Toss bread with vegetables. Add stock gradually until moist but not soggy; taste and season before adding eggs. Stir in beaten eggs.

  4. Spread in a buttered baking dish. Cover and bake 25 minutes, then uncover and bake 15–25 minutes until golden and center reaches 165°F.

Kitchen wisdom

Watch the consistency: the bread should be moist, not swimming in stock.

Pecan Pie

SERVES / YIELD • 8 | 15 min prep | 55 min bake

Ingredients

  • 1 unbaked 9-inch pie crust

  • 1 cup light or dark corn syrup

  • ¾ cup brown sugar

  • 3 eggs

  • 3 tbsp melted butter

  • 1 tsp vanilla

  • 1½ cups pecans

  • Pinch of salt

Method

  1. Heat oven to 350°F. Whisk syrup, sugar, eggs, butter, vanilla and salt.

  2. Arrange pecans in crust and pour filling over them.

  3. Bake 50–60 minutes until edges are set and center reaches 200°F. Shield crust if browning too fast.

  4. Cool on a rack and refrigerate within 2 hours.

Kitchen wisdom

Let the pie cool before slicing so the filling has time to set.
